Ethan Qin Zhou

I am a junior Industrial Distribution student at Texas A&M, and I came into this class thinking MIS was just another "business course," which turned out to be the wrong framing in almost every way.
I chose ID after a summer selling construction supplies at Bellevue Builders Depot, where doing material takeoffs showed me that the real problem-solving happens in getting the right product to the right jobsite at the right time.
What I actually learned here is that the systems side of a distributor is not support, it is the reason the rest of the company works, because inventory, pricing, and customer data only matter if the systems behind them are right.
Researching WannaCry showed me the other half of that, because a patch Microsoft had already released could not protect hospitals that never installed it, so keeping systems updated is as much a part of running a business as keeping inventory accurate.
I use AI to talk myself through concepts I keep misremembering, but the line I draw is this: I am allowed to ask for help understanding the piece I am stuck on, I am not allowed to replace the piece I was supposed to do the thinking on.
What I am taking from this class is not a set of answers, it is a habit of asking "who decides what this is for" before trusting a report or a system, and I think that habit will outlast whatever I remember about specific tools.
